Understanding Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is a progressive medical condition where the kidneys gradually lose their vital ability to filter wastes, toxins, and excess fluids from the blood. Healthy kidneys process roughly 120 to 150 quarts of blood daily to maintain essential electrolyte and biochemical balance.

Often termed a "silent disease", CKD frequently exhibits few or no noticeable symptoms during its early stages (Stages 1–3). Routine biochemical screening via serum creatinine, eGFR, urine albumin, and clinical indicators provides the earliest window to halt or delay disease progression.
